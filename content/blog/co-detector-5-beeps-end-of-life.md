“Five beeps” is not a universal carbon-monoxide alarm code. Manufacturers use different sound patterns, displays, lights, and voice alerts for low battery, end of life, tamper warnings, faults, and CO events. Do not decide that a pattern is harmless—or that it definitely means end of life—without the exact make and model.

**If the alarm is loud and repeating, or you cannot tell whether it is an active CO alarm, get everyone outside to fresh air and call emergency services from outside.** The [CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Education-Centers/Carbon-Monoxide-Information-Center/Carbon-Monoxide-Questions-and-Answers) says not to search for the source before leaving and not to re-enter until responders say it is safe.

## What to do after the immediate safety step

Once you have confirmed with emergency responders or the product documentation that the unit is not issuing a CO alarm:

1. Read the label for the manufacturer, model, power source, manufacture date, and stated replacement age.
2. Look up the current manual or manufacturer support page for that exact model.
3. Check for an error message, indicator light, or replacement date that accompanies the sound.
4. Replace the unit if the manual identifies an end-of-life or non-serviceable fault signal.

Kidde's current CO support illustrates why a generic count is unsafe: it describes a four-short-beep alarm for CO on its models, while chirps can be low battery, end of life, malfunction, or a display error. Other manufacturers may assign different patterns.

## Do not make a safety device quiet by disabling it

Do not pull a battery, unplug a unit, or cover the speaker and leave the home without CO protection. If a correct fresh battery and the manufacturer-approved procedure do not resolve a documented maintenance signal, replace the unit or contact the manufacturer. A test button checks basic circuitry, not the accuracy of the CO sensor.

## FAQ

### Does five beeps always mean a CO detector is expired?

No. A beep count alone is not a universal end-of-life code. Check the exact model manual and any display or light on the unit.

### Can I reset the alarm to clear five beeps?

Use only the reset steps published for your model and only after you have ruled out an active CO alarm. A reset cannot restore an expired or faulty sensor.
