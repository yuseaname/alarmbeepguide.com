# CO Detector 5 Beeps End of Life: Replacement Timeline and Best Options

Five beeps from a carbon monoxide detector typically signals end of life. The CO sensor inside the detector has a finite lifespan of 5-7 years, after which it must be replaced. This guide explains why CO detectors expire faster than smoke detectors, what the 5-beep pattern means, and how to choose a replacement CO detector in 2026.

## Why 5 Beeps Means End of Life

CO detectors use a 5-beep pattern to signal that the CO sensor has expired. This pattern is different from other CO detector beeps:

- **4 loud beeps (with pauses):** Active CO alarm — dangerous CO levels
- **5 beeps (pattern varies):** End of life — sensor has expired

The 5-beep pattern indicates that the electrochemical CO sensor inside the detector has degraded beyond reliable operation. No amount of battery replacement or reset will fix this — the entire detector must be replaced.

## Why CO Sensors Have a Shorter Lifespan Than Smoke Sensors

CO detectors expire after 5-7 years, while smoke detectors last 10 years. The reason is the type of sensor used:

### Electrochemical CO Sensors

CO detectors use an electrochemical cell to detect carbon monoxide. The cell contains a chemical electrolyte that reacts with CO, generating a small electrical current. The detector measures this current to determine CO levels.

Over time, the electrolyte degrades. After 5-7 years, the cell can no longer reliably generate enough current to detect CO. The sensor must be replaced.

### Photoelectric and Ionization Smoke Sensors

Smoke detectors use either photoelectric (light-based) or ionization (radioactive source) sensors. These sensors degrade more slowly than electrochemical cells. After 10 years, they still work, but their reliability decreases.

### Different Sensors, Different Lifespans

Because of the sensor type, CO detectors have a shorter lifespan than smoke detectors. Most manufacturers set the CO detector end-of-life at 5-7 years.

## How to Check Your CO Detector's Expiration Date

Every CO detector has a manufacture date and an expiration date printed on the back.

### Finding the Date

1. Remove the detector from its mounting bracket
2. Look at the back for a label
3. Find the "Manufactured" or "MFG" date
4. Find the "Replace by" or "EXP" date

The "Replace by" date is 5-7 years after the manufacture date, depending on the manufacturer.

### If You Can't Find the Date

Some older detectors have faded labels. If you can't find the date:

1. Check the model number on the manufacturer's website
2. Look for the date inside the battery compartment
3. If you still can't find it, assume the detector is expired and replace it

## What to Do When Your CO Detector Sounds 5 Beeps

### Step 1: Verify It's End of Life

Before replacing the detector, verify that the 5-beep pattern is end of life and not another issue:

- Check the manufacture date on the back
- If the date is 5-7+ years ago, it's end of life
- If the date is recent, check for other issues (low battery, sensor fault)

### Step 2: Silence the Alarm Temporarily

Press and hold the test button for 5 seconds. The beeping will be silenced for 8-12 hours, giving you time to buy a replacement.

### Step 3: Buy a Replacement

Purchase a replacement CO detector. Consider:

- Battery-only vs. hardwired
- CO-only vs. combination smoke/CO
- Smart features
- 10-year sealed battery

### Step 4: Install the New Detector

1. Remove the old detector
2. Install the new detector in the same location (or improved location)
3. Test the new detector
4. Properly dispose of the old detector

### Step 5: Reset Your Calendar

Set a reminder for 5-7 years from now to replace the new detector. CO detector end-of-life is easy to forget without a reminder.

## Where to Install CO Detectors

NFPA 720 recommends CO detectors in these locations:

- Outside each sleeping area
- On every level of the home
- Near combustion appliances (furnace, water heater, gas stove)

Avoid placing CO detectors:
- In dead air spaces (corners, peaks of vaulted ceilings)
- Behind furniture or curtains
- Near windows or doors (drafts can affect readings)
- In bathrooms (humidity can damage the sensor)
- In direct sunlight (heat can damage the sensor)

For full placement guidance, see our [Carbon Monoxide Detector Placement Guide](/blog/carbon-monoxide-detector-placement-guide/).

## CO Detector Placement After Replacement

When you replace your CO detector, consider whether your placement was adequate. If your expired detector was far from where a CO leak could occur, add additional detectors.

### Recommended Coverage

For most US homes, install CO detectors:

- Outside each bedroom (in the hallway)
- In the basement near the furnace
- In the kitchen or near the gas stove (at least 10 feet from the stove)
- In the room with the water heater

This typically requires 3-5 CO detectors depending on the home's layout.

## Disposing of Old CO Detectors

Don't throw expired CO detectors in the regular trash. They contain electronic components and (in some models) small batteries that need special disposal.

### Recycling Options

- **Home Depot and Lowe's:** Have e-waste recycling programs that accept CO detectors
- **Manufacturer mail-back:** Some manufacturers offer mail-back recycling
- **Local e-waste facilities:** Most cities have e-waste recycling facilities
- **Hazardous waste facilities:** Some CO detectors have small batteries that require hazardous waste disposal

Check your local regulations for specific disposal requirements.

## How to Remember the Replacement Date

CO detectors expire every 5-7 years. It's easy to forget without a reminder. Here are some strategies:

### Calendar Reminder

Set a calendar reminder for 5 years from the install date. The reminder should tell you to:
- Check the CO detector's manufacture date
- Verify the replace-by date
- Plan a replacement purchase

### Phone App

Some smart CO detectors (First Alert SC5, Nest Protect) track their own expiration date and notify you through the app. If you have a smart detector, let it remind you.

### Physical Note

Write the replacement date on the inside of the battery compartment with a permanent marker. When you check the detector, you'll see the date.

### Replace on Your Birthday

A common strategy is to replace CO detectors every 5 years on a memorable date (your birthday, New Year's Day, etc.). This makes it easy to remember.

## CO Detector vs. Combination Smoke/CO Detector

When replacing your expired CO detector, consider whether to upgrade to a combination smoke/CO detector.

### Advantages of Combination

- Two safety devices in one
- Lower cost than buying separate detectors
- Single installation
- Single battery replacement

### Disadvantages of Combination

- CO sensor expires at 5-7 years; smoke sensor at 10 years
- When the CO sensor expires, the entire unit must be replaced
- May not detect smoke and CO at the same sensitivity as separate units

For most homes, a combination smoke/CO detector is the better choice. The cost savings and convenience outweigh the shorter CO sensor lifespan.

## Signs That Your CO Detector Needs Replacement (Beyond 5 Beeps)

Even before the 5-beep end-of-life signal, your CO detector may need replacement if:

### The Display Shows Erratic Readings

A digital CO detector should show 0 ppm when no CO is present. Erratic readings (jumping between 10, 30, 0, 25) indicate a failing sensor.

### The Test Button Doesn't Work

Press the test button. The alarm should sound. If it doesn't, the detector has a fault.

### The Detector Is Yellowed or Discolored

Plastic discoloration from UV exposure can indicate that the detector is aging. Replace it.

### The Detector Has Been Recalled

Check for active recalls on your CO detector brand. Replace immediately if recalled.

### The Detector Has Been Exposed to High CO Levels

If the detector sounded during a CO incident, the sensor may be damaged. Replace the detector after a serious CO event.

## CO Detector Maintenance

CO detectors need minimal maintenance, but these steps extend their lifespan:

### Monthly Testing

Press the test button once a month. The alarm should sound.

### Annual Cleaning

Remove the detector once a year and clean it with a soft cloth. Don't use cleaning chemicals or solvents.

### Replacement Schedule

Replace the detector at the end of its 5-7 year lifespan, or sooner if it shows signs of failure.

## FAQ

### What does 5 beeps on a CO detector mean?

Five beeps typically indicates end of life. The CO sensor has expired and the detector must be replaced.

### Is 5 beeps dangerous?

Five beeps is not dangerous by itself — it's a maintenance alert. However, the CO sensor is no longer functioning, so the detector cannot alert you to actual CO. Replace the detector promptly.

### Can I reset the 5-beep end-of-life signal?

No. The 5-beep end-of-life signal indicates that the sensor has failed and cannot be reset. Replace the detector.

### How long do CO detectors last?

5-7 years for CO-only detectors. 10 years for combination smoke/CO detectors, but the CO sensor may need replacement sooner.

### Why do CO detectors expire sooner than smoke detectors?

CO sensors use electrochemical cells that degrade faster than the photoelectric or ionization sensors used in smoke detectors.

### Can I just replace the CO sensor module?

Some combination detectors have replaceable CO sensor modules. Most CO-only detectors do not. Check your model.

### How do I find the manufacture date on my CO detector?

Look on the back of the detector for a label that says "Manufactured" or "MFG" followed by a date. The replace-by date is 5-7 years after the manufacture date.

### What if my CO detector is 4 years old and chirping?

Check the chirp pattern. If it's 1 chirp every 60 seconds, it's low battery. If it's 5 beeps, it may be an early end-of-life signal. Contact the manufacturer.

### Should I replace all CO detectors at the same time?

If they were all installed at the same time, yes. This ensures consistent replacement cycles.

### Can I dispose of old CO detectors in regular trash?

No. CO detectors contain electronic components and should be recycled through e-waste programs.
