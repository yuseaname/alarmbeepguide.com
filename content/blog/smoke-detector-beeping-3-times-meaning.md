A continuing set of three loud beeps—**beep, beep, beep, pause, then repeat**—should be treated as a smoke or fire alarm. The [National Fire Protection Association](https://www.nfpa.org/education-and-research/home-fire-safety/smoke-alarms) says to get out, call 911, and stay out. Do not remain inside to decide whether the smoke is visible, whether cooking caused it, or whether the sound will stop.

If the unit is a combination smoke/CO alarm, a different loud pattern can also indicate carbon monoxide. When there is uncertainty, leave first and follow emergency guidance.

## What to do now

1. **Get everyone outside.** Take people and pets with you and use your home fire-escape plan.
2. **Call 911 or your local emergency number from outside.**
3. **Stay out** until emergency responders say it is safe to return.
4. **Do not disable the alarm** by removing its battery, disconnecting it, or covering it.

A loud smoke-alarm pattern is not the same as a single periodic chirp. An unfamiliar sound can still be urgent, so use the product manual only after the immediate safety step.

## If the sound is not a loud repeating alarm

An isolated chirp, a display code, or a different cadence can mean battery, end-of-life, fault, or another model-specific condition. It is not safe to label those meanings from the number “three” alone. Check the alarm's label for its make and model, then consult the current manufacturer instructions.

Kidde's current smoke-alarm support, for example, distinguishes loud alarming, consistent chirping, intermittent chirping, and certain smart-alarm updates. Your device may use different sounds or lights. For a short maintenance chirp after you have ruled out an active alarm, see [Smoke Alarm Chirping: Check the Battery, Date, and Manual](/blog/smoke-detector-chirping-low-battery/).

## Do not use a generic reset sequence

A reset may be appropriate for some models after a battery change or power interruption, but steps differ between battery-only, hardwired, interconnected, and combination alarms. Use the model manual. If wiring is damaged, a circuit breaker trips repeatedly, or the unit has reached its replacement date, stop troubleshooting and contact a qualified electrician, manufacturer, or service professional.

For a hardwired unit, use [Hardwired Smoke Alarm Chirping: Safe Troubleshooting](/blog/hardwired-smoke-detector-beeping-troubleshooting/). For a combination device, use [Combination Smoke + CO Alarm Beeping](/blog/combo-smoke-co-alarm-beeping-patterns/).

## FAQ

### Does three beeps mean a fire even when I cannot see smoke?

Treat a continued loud three-beep pattern as a smoke or fire alarm. Smoke can be hidden or develop quickly. Get out, call 911, and stay out.

### What if the beeps stop after I leave?

Stay outside and follow emergency-responder direction. A quiet alarm does not establish that the home is safe to re-enter.

### Does a test button make the same sound?

A test can produce a loud alarm sound, but it checks basic circuitry; it does not prove that the sensor is responding to real smoke. Follow the manufacturer's test instructions.
