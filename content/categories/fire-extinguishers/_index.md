A fire extinguisher is a tool for a small, contained fire—not a reason to delay escaping a dangerous one. Keep an exit behind you, call 911, and leave immediately if the fire grows, smoke makes it hard to see or breathe, or you are unsure which extinguisher to use. The [U.S. Fire Administration](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prepare/fire-extinguishers/) is a useful starting point for home fire-extinguisher readiness.

## Start with the label, not a guess

The label identifies the extinguisher's fire classes and operating instructions. Do not use water on a grease fire or attempt to fight an electrical fire with an extinguisher that is not rated for it. If you have not been trained or cannot keep a clear escape route, get out and let the fire department handle it.

## Keep it usable without treating inspection as repair

A monthly look can catch an extinguisher that is blocked, missing, visibly damaged, or showing an abnormal gauge position. Do not tamper with a safety seal, nozzle, or pressure valve. If it has been used even briefly, is corroded, leaks, has a damaged hose, or has an unclear service history, follow the manufacturer instructions or contact a qualified service provider.

Use the [monthly home inspection checklist](/blog/fire-extinguisher-inspection-checklist/) to make that check consistent. For a practical location review, see [fire-extinguisher mounting height and placement](/blog/fire-extinguisher-mounting-height-placement/).

## Put escape first

An extinguisher works best when it is easy to reach on the way out and everyone in the home understands the escape plan. Test smoke alarms regularly, keep exits clear, and call emergency services for any fire.
